Joy found the boat near the old harbor on a cool, wet morning. Her younger brother Roy pointed at a
pair of strange birds that curled around the mast. "Look," he whispered, "they're choosing a
perch." The usual noise of the fishing fleet had stopped; only the hum of an oily engine and the
measured splash of oars reached the shore. Joy pushed her hands deep into her pockets, enjoyed the
quiet, and thought about the treasure hidden somewhere beyond the jagged rocks. Their father, a
careful captain with a thick beard, shouted that the tide would turn by noon. Nobody wanted to
argue or make conversation. They loaded six heavy crates, a bucket of bright yellow paint, and a
coil of thin rope into the hold. As the vessel moved out, the sky changed from pale gray to a vivid
purple blue, and a sudden breeze flung cold spray across the deck. "Why, Joy, how odd," said Roy.
"We see oysters everywhere!" Roy laughed, climbed the ladder, and sang a cheerful song about
pirates, pleasure, and a cure for boredom. Unexpectedly, the whole voyage became a particularly
extraordinary opportunity. Joy smiled; this adventure was precisely what she had imagined.
